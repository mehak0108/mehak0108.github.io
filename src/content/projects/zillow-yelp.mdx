## Problem

Standing alone, both companies carry structural exposure. Yelp depends on third-party discovery — it ranks 7th on Google Search for local business queries, monetizes almost exclusively through advertising, and faces encroaching substitutes in Apple Maps, Google Maps, and Meta. Zillow has zero recurring revenue: monetization ends the moment a transaction closes, engagement drops sharply afterward, and its 5-year revenue CAGR sits at -4%, tied tightly to housing-cycle swings reflected in its 1.55 equity beta.

The question we set out to answer: is there a single acquirer whose core strength cures the other's core weakness, and can that combination be priced and structured defensibly enough to take to a board?

## Solution

We screened alternative acquirers first — Booking.com (travel intent doesn't transfer to local home-services demand) and Gartner (a B2B firm with no consumer traffic or SMB advertiser base) — and rejected both before building the case for Zillow, whose 228M+ high-intent home-shopper traffic is exactly what Yelp's services marketplace lacks an owned channel into, while Yelp's 308M+ reviews and SMB network give Zillow the recurring engagement layer it has never had.

We valued Yelp standalone via DCF at $1.87B (WACC 11.83%, equity beta 0.88), then built a separate synergy DCF modeling three categories of value: customer-facing (home-services lead routing, a bundled "Moving Bundle," Yelp content embedded in Zillow's map), tech (AI-driven neighborhood insights, consolidated data infrastructure), and operational (SG&A elimination, reduced CAC). The two largest synergy lines — home-services uplift at $360M and the moving bundle at $265M — anchored a $625M Year-1 incremental revenue base and a $630M 3-year synergy NPV, bringing total recommended price to $2.5B, a 33.6% premium over Yelp's standalone value and squarely inside the typical 19–36% M&A premium range.

We then worked through the accounting mechanics (purchase price allocation across $902M goodwill and $1.05B in intangibles), built a risk mitigation plan addressing the real failure modes of M&A (1 in 2 acquired employees leave by year 2; 57% of deals fail to create meaningful synergies), and sequenced a four-phase, 36-month integration roadmap — due diligence, cross-data population, "super app" bundling, then marketplace maintenance — designed to protect revenue capture while minimizing regulatory and culture-clash risk.

## Outcome

The completed business case presented a fully reasoned $2.5B acquisition proposal: a valuation grounded in DCF and comparable premium analysis, a synergy model broken into customer-facing, tech, and cost categories with revenue traced to specific mechanisms rather than a top-down multiplier, full purchase price allocation and intangible amortization schedules, and a phased implementation plan with named risk mitigations at each stage — delivered as a formal board-style proposal for Paw Club Advisors at the Foster School of Business.
