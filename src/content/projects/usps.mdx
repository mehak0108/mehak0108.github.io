## Problem

A USPS retail location was visibly congested at peak hours, but nobody could say exactly why, or what to do about it, without data. We ran a live site visit and timed every stage of the customer journey: inter-arrival times, processing times at staff counters versus self-service kiosks, queue lengths, and throughput. The numbers confirmed what the lobby felt like: counter service was running at 96.5% utilization with only 2–3 employees, average wait time sat at 4 minutes 4 seconds, and the queue regularly held over 6 customers. Eighty percent of all arrivals were funneling into the constrained counter line even though self-service kiosks sat underused at just 10–11% of volume.

The question wasn't whether there was a bottleneck, it was visible, it was whether fixing it required adding headcount, or whether the existing capacity in the self-service kiosks was simply being routed around.

## Solution

We built a process simulator calibrated to the observed data (95 customers/hour arrival rate, staff-counter processing time of 2:17, self-service processing time of 4:14) to test interventions before recommending any of them. The root-cause analysis pointed to a behavioral problem, not a structural one: customers were confused about which transactions qualified for self-service, where to affix labels, and whether the kiosks could handle their needs, so eligible customers defaulted to the counter line by habit rather than necessity.

We modeled four scenarios against the baseline. Simply adding a self-service station barely moved the needle (wait time dropped from 4:04 to 3:38). Adding a fourth counter worked dramatically (wait time fell to 14 seconds) but came with a permanent labor cost. The scenario that mattered: increasing self-service routing from 11% to 20%, a 9-percentage-point shift achievable purely through signage and a better kiosk experience, cut average wait time to 1 minute 24 seconds and utilization to 84.5%, with zero additional headcount.

## Outcome

We recommended redirecting eligible customers to self-service through clear entrance signage and an improved kiosk experience (stocked supplies, simpler instructions, reliable uptime), rather than adding a fourth counter. The simulation showed this single behavioral fix delivers a 65% reduction in average wait time using capacity that already exists. Next steps handed to the location: install signage, track self-service utilization before/after, and if routing reaches the modeled 20%, formalize kiosk supply protocols as standard operating procedure.
