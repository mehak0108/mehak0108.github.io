Executive Summary

When Mishipay's account POC for Grandiose UAE resigned without notice, transaction discrepancies across 5 stores were left without an owner, threatening a key Middle East contract. I stepped in voluntarily, leading a structured 15-day investigation across three organizations, two technical systems, and a high-stakes client relationship under active escalation.

Across the 5 stores, Grandiose was processing approximately 5,000 transactions per day through Network International's payment gateway. A ~12% discrepancy rate meant roughly 600 orders every day were being marked as failed on Mishipay's side despite being successfully settled by NI, creating a growing gap between what Grandiose's finance team saw and what Mishipay's platform reported.

Transaction mismatches between order records in Mishipay's platform and settlement data held by NI (the third-party payment gateway) were eroding Grandiose's confidence in the self-checkout deployment. Without resolution, the client risked reverting to traditional checkout and Mishipay risked losing its largest UAE contract.

By designing a rigorous Python-based data reconciliation approach, coordinating root cause analysis across three stakeholder groups, and communicating proactively with the client throughout, all discrepancies were resolved within 15 days. The outcome exceeded expectations: Grandiose expanded the engagement from 5 to 25 stores, generating $380K in additional annual revenue and preventing $140K in contract losses.

Background & Context

Mishipay

Mishipay provides AI-powered self-checkout solutions enabling shoppers to scan, pay, and leave without queuing. Operating across the US, UK, Canada and Middle East, Mishipay integrates with existing retail infrastructure, POS systems, payment gateways, and inventory management, to deliver frictionless checkout at scale. Grandiose UAE was one of Mishipay's most strategically important deployments in the region.

Grandiose — UAE Premium Supermarket Chain

Grandiose is a premium supermarket chain operating high-footfall stores across the UAE, with locations in residential and commercial hubs including Sports City and Binghatti Rose. At approximately 1,000 transactions per store per day, the 5-store Mishipay deployment processed around 5,000 daily transactions through Network International (NI), a leading payment technology provider in the Middle East that handled card authorization, settlement, and financial reporting on Grandiose's behalf.

The Challenge

Unlike a single-system bug, this investigation involved three distinct data owners, each maintaining its own transaction records, and each capable of being the source of discrepancy. Resolving the issue required simultaneous access, alignment, and trust from all three parties at a moment when client confidence was already fragile:

  • Mishipay: Transaction initiation, basket composition, split-tender processing, and order records
  • Network International (NI – payment provider): Card authorization, settlement processing, and financial reporting to Grandiose
  • Grandiose IT: End-of-day reconciliation, internal financial systems, and daily settlement sign-off

The risk of misattributing root cause to the wrong party, or shipping a premature fix, could have extended the timeline and further damaged all three relationships. A structured investigation was essential.

Technical Approach & Key Decisions

Mapping Unfamiliar Territory First

This investigation required ramping up on a domain I had not previously worked in, Mishipay's internal payment architecture. Before writing any reconciliation scripts or forming hypotheses, I engaged the payments team directly to understand how a transaction moved through the system end-to-end: from the SCO kiosk through the Mishipay API, into the transaction database, and across to Network International's settlement layer. This was my first time tracing a full payment transaction flow, and I treated the knowledge-gathering phase as seriously as the technical investigation itself. Understanding the system accurately before analyzing it was what made the root cause identifiable at all.

Data Reconciliation with Python

The investigation's analytical backbone was a Python reconciliation script that fetched day-wise transaction records from Mishipay's backend API and cross-matched them against Network International's settlement files. The script automated three core checks:

  • Count verification: Orders initiated on Mishipay vs. orders settled by NI, per store per day
  • Amount matching: Transaction totals before and after Network International processing to surface value gaps
  • Status reconciliation: Identification of orphaned transactions — recorded as failed on Mishipay but present and settled in NI's ledger

The analysis revealed a consistent, reproducible pattern: payments that Network International had fully processed and settled were not being reflected as successful in Mishipay's order records. The root cause was a status code issue, NI's API was intermittently returning a non-success status code to Mishipay even when the underlying payment had processed correctly. Mishipay's system, reading the incorrect status, would mark the order as incomplete, creating a phantom discrepancy between what Network International had settled and what Mishipay had recorded. The fix required updates on both sides: Network International corrected their API to return accurate status codes on successful transactions, and Mishipay implemented retry and reconciliation logic to re-poll NI's API and verify the true transaction status rather than trusting the initial response.

Multi-Party Stakeholder Coordination

Managing three organizations simultaneously during a live client investigation required deliberate communication design. A shared issue tracker mapped each discrepancy type to its owning team, proposed fix, and status, visible to Grandiose IT, Network International, and Mishipay's backend team. A single weekly cross-party sync replaced separate bilateral calls, reducing coordination overhead and ensuring that no one party could shift accountability without group visibility. Client updates to Grandiose were structured around confirmed milestones ("Stores 1 and 2 are now fully reconciled") rather than open-ended timelines, this rebuilt trust incrementally rather than asking for patience until a complete resolution.

Key Decision: Communicate Early or Wait for Full Resolution

A core judgment call was whether to share partial findings with the client as they emerged, or wait for a complete picture. Sharing preliminary data too early risked creating confusion if findings changed; waiting 15 days would signal disengagement during a period of active client anxiety. The middle path chosen, sharing confirmed findings store-by-store as each was validated, preserved credibility and gave the client visible evidence of progress. This turned a 15-day investigation into a series of milestone moments.

Process Improvement: Reactive to Proactive

Beyond resolving the immediate issue, the reconciliation process was designed for ongoing use. A daily monitoring script was automated to alert on settlement count divergence above a defined threshold, shifting from client-reported detection to proactive identification within 24 hours. SOPs were authored covering reconciliation steps, failure modes, and escalation paths, then handed off via a structured knowledge transfer session to a junior developer who assumed ongoing ownership.

Outcomes & Business Impact

Beyond the Numbers

The impact of this project extended beyond its direct financial metrics. Grandiose's decision to expand to 25 stores was a signal that proactive, transparent ownership during the crisis had strengthened the relationship beyond its pre-crisis baseline. The automated reconciliation framework built during this investigation became a reference implementation adopted for other high-volume UAE clients, multiplying its value across Mishipay's Middle East portfolio.

This project also illustrated how engineering-led product thinking—structuring a complex investigation with clear phases, measurable milestones, and stakeholder-appropriate communication—can drive significant commercial outcomes in a startup environment where role boundaries are fluid and ownership is earned.

Lessons Learned

1. Proactive ownership is a competitive differentiator

In a fast-scaling startup, gaps in account ownership surface regularly. Stepping into ambiguity without being asked, especially on a high-visibility crisis, built credibility with both internal leadership and the client simultaneously. The reputational risk of owning a broken client situation was lower than the cost of waiting for assignment while the relationship deteriorated.

2. Structured investigation outperforms fast guessing

The instinct in a live client crisis is to ship fixes immediately. The right first move was asking "who understands this system?" not "what's broken?" I had never traced a payment transaction flow before, and spending time with Mishipay's payments team before touching any data was what made the investigation tractable. From there, disciplined phase-gating, mapping data ownership before writing scripts, building reconciliation logic before forming hypotheses, validating against historical data before communicating, prevented incorrect fixes that could have extended the timeline. Slowing down in the early phases significantly accelerated the overall resolution.

3. Communication cadence rebuilds trust incrementally

Client confidence was restored through a series of milestone-based updates across 15 days, not through a single resolution announcement at the end. Structuring each client touchpoint around what had been confirmed, store by store, made 15 days of investigation feel like a series of forward moments rather than a silence followed by a resolution. Trust is rebuilt in installments.
